01 · Executive summary

Quebecers pay ~$2.1 billion a year in commissions.
Clé replaces the middle with a platform.

Quebec recorded 97,214 residential sales in 2025 — a third-best year on record — at a median of $491,500 for a single-family home.1 A typical ~5% commission means $24,600 per median sale, an implied provincial commission pool of $1.9–2.35 billion annually.1,4,11 Quebec is already Canada's most commission-skeptical market: 15–20% of homes sell without a broker, the highest share in the country.2

DuProprio proved the model — $45.6M revenue, positive EBITDA, an NPS of 61 versus the industry's 38 — but stalled as a one-product company, changing hands three times at a flat $50–60M valuation.3 The lesson is not that the opportunity is small; it is that a listing package alone has a ceiling.

Clé stacks five engines on one verified network so every user compounds the value of the next: owner-direct listings (proven demand), professional subscriptions (LinkedIn economics), group property purchase (the co-ownership wave institutional capital is already backing — Ourboro alone has co-invested in 250+ homes with Scotiabank and CMHC behind it7), a regulated capital platform (exempt-market dealer economics), and discreet, capped advertising. A fully interactive, trilingual prototype with integrated AI is built and demonstrable today — live at ssocciarelli.github.io/cle-demo. The founding team and advisors are presented in §10; the plan states its pre-launch status plainly in §05.

Why now

  • Affordability is forcing collective ownership. Median Montréal single-family prices rose 9% in 2025 to $622,5001; co-buying platforms (Ourboro, Key, Pacaso's US$72.5M round) show institutional conviction that pooled ownership is the next primitive.7
  • The FSBO incumbent is a harvest asset. DuProprio, owned by a bank-insurer since 2020, has not shipped a second act; its own customers are the beachhead for a broader platform.3
  • Retail capital is structurally locked out. Canada's entire start-up-crowdfunding channel moved only ~$5M in its best year5 — not for lack of demand (US peers: Arrived US$340M, Fundrise US$3.3B AUM6) but for lack of a trusted, compliant consumer front-end. That is a product problem, and product problems are venture opportunities.
  • Québec subsidizes exactly this build. CDAEIA e-business tax credits (20–30%), Impulsion PME matching, and the $200M Fonds Impulsion lower the effective cost of our engineering roadmap.8
03 · The product

Five engines, one verified network — built and clickable today

Clé is not a concept deck. A fully interactive prototype — trilingual (FR/EN/IT), AI-assisted, with every flow working end-to-end — exists now and is demonstrated live in investor meetings.

1 · Proprio-direct (sell without commission)
Fixed packages at $1,199 / $1,599 / $1,999 mirroring the market-validated ladder3: pro photos, coaching, FOR-SALE sign, notary on file. Listing wizard with AI pricing assessment, comparables-based suggestions, real photo upload, seller dashboard with leads, views and 48-hour follow-up nudges.
2 · Marketplace
Properties from $450K condos to $40M development sites; confidential business-for-sale listings gated behind e-signed NDAs with logged access; verified brokers and notaries attached to every file; favorites, alerts, price history, neighbourhood scores, Canadian-formula mortgage calculator.
3 · Buy groups (achat en groupe)
Start a purchase consortium on any listing: pooled down payments, 49.9% per-member cap, double-majority governance per art. 1026 C.c.Q., notarized co-ownership agreement, funds in notary trust, group offer flowing into a secure deal room.
4 · Capital platform
Revenue-share campaigns from $100/unit under NI 45-110 (caps, all-or-nothing, 48-h withdrawal, risk forms — all modeled in-product), institutional-grade structured offerings, a financing wizard that routes each project to its correct legal lane, and a portfolio view with distribution schedules and annual buy-back windows.
5 · Professional network
LinkedIn-style profiles with a three-level verification ladder culminating in licence checks against OACIQ, OIQ, OAQ, CNQ, Barreau and RBQ registries; feed, messaging, quote requests, consultations — and discreet, hard-capped native advertising (at most 1 sponsored post in 6).
Clé IA
AI woven through the product: natural-language search ("condo à Rosemont sous 600k" → parsed, answered, linked), instant property assessments grounded in comparables, neighbourhood data and uploaded photos, and pricing guidance for sellers. Production architecture: Claude API with vision.

A working demonstration is available at the private link supplied with this document. Every figure and profile in the prototype is fictional; every control works.

Key assumptions

  • Listings: 300 → 12,000 paid listings by FY31 at a $1,500 average package (benchmark: the incumbent's FY2017 revenue per listing of ≈$1,170, ≈$1,500 in today's dollars3). FY31 volume equals 62–82% of Quebec's current 14,600–19,400 annual FSBO sales2 — explicitly a displacement of DuProprio's Quebec base — plus Ontario contribution from FY30. At a more conservative 35% share of Quebec FSBO with no Ontario, FY31 owner-direct revenue is ≈$8.6M and breakeven moves ~3 quarters into FY31.
  • Fulfillment: $340 per listing for photography, signage and coaching time through a volume partner network — modeled as a direct cost line, not buried.
  • Subscriptions: 200 → 4,600 Pro members ($468/yr) against ~20,000+ licensed brokers, notaries, inspectors and contractors in Quebec; 130 firm accounts by FY31; 33%/yr churn hypothesis pending cohort data.
  • Capital platform: GMV $2M (FY28, partner portal) → $40M (FY31, own EMD) at a blended 5% placement fee + 0.75% trailing administration. FY31 lane split: ~$4M under 45-110 (crowdfunding tier — comparable to today's entire national channel5), ~$26M via offering-memorandum raises and ~$10M accredited — the lanes where EMD economics actually live and where no issuer caps apply. For scale: FrontFundr processed $285M over ten years nationally5; our plan assumes Quebec category leadership, not US-style volumes.
  • Costs: Montréal fully-loaded average of $118K/FTE; marketing declining from 50% to 20% of revenue as the flywheel compounds; the compliance line carries the EMD build (capital, CCO, audits) from FY29.5
  • Not modeled (upside): CDAEIA tax credits (20–30% of eligible dev salaries), Impulsion PME matching, mortgage-referral revenue, business-listing NDA fees.8
Sensitivity. If the capital platform is delayed a full year (EMD friction), FY31 revenue falls ≈$1.5M to $23.0M and FY30 breakeven slips ~2 quarters — the listing and subscription engines still carry the company. No single engine failure is fatal; that is the point of five.

The Purplebricks question, pre-empted. The best-known fixed-fee experiment collapsed and sold for £1 in the UK. Its failure modes are specific: cash-burning multi-country expansion, a salaried hybrid-agent payroll, and a market with no FSBO culture. Quebec is the opposite setup — a 15–20% FSBO share built over 25 years2, no agent payroll in our model (partner-fulfilled packages), and a single-province beachhead. DuProprio's decade of positive EBITDA in this exact market3 is the counter-example that Quebec fixed-fee works.

If Desjardins responds — a 30% package price cut costs DuProprio ≈$14M of annual revenue on FY2017 volumes to defend a single product line; Clé's contribution comes from five streams, so matching that cut costs us ≈7% of FY31 revenue. Their five-year shipping record under bank ownership is the practical moat; our answer to a network feature launch is 18 months of head start on verification, buy-groups and the capital stack.

The FSBO objection, answered. Broker-funded studies claim private sales fetch 15–20% less.10 Clé's counter is structural: AI-grounded pricing against live comparables, mandatory notary involvement, minimum photo standards, negotiation coaching, and buyer-side verification — the exact failure modes those studies exploit, productized away. DuProprio's FY2017 NPS of 61 versus the industry's 38 shows which side customers believed.3

08 · Regulatory strategy

Compliance as product architecture, not paperwork

  • Brokerage boundary (OACIQ). Owner-direct selling is legal in Quebec; the platform advertises and coaches but never represents or negotiates. DuProprio litigated and validated this line; we operate inside it with a standing legal opinion.
  • Active co-purchase ≠ securities. Buy-group members hold title or voting LP units and govern by double majority (art. 1026 C.c.Q.)12 — profits do not depend on others' efforts, keeping groups outside distribution rules. The product enforces the guardrails: caps, votes, notarized agreements, notary trust accounts.
  • Capital in three lanes. ≤$1.5M public raises under NI 45-110 through a registered portal (investor caps $2,500/$10,000, all-or-nothing, 48-h withdrawal, F1/F2 forms — French-first in Quebec); larger raises via offering memorandum; accredited rounds uncapped. Revenue-share is delivered through limited-partnership units — an eligible security — never bare royalty contracts.5
  • The addy lesson, internalized. addy's $100K penalty for pre-registration trading9 defines our sequencing: Phase 1 earns revenue with zero securities exposure; Phase 2 rides a registered partner; Phase 3 registers Clé Capital inc. as an EMD ($50K capital, designated CCO, audited filings5) inside a separate entity that never touches platform operations.
  • Privacy & language. Law 25 program (privacy officer, PIAs, consent registry) and Charter-compliant French-first product — both already reflected in the prototype.
